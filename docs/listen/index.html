grooveback listening — published packs

Every track is level-matched to −14 LUFS with one common headroom gain per player, master included. Solo is exclusive: keys 1–9 or a click switch tracks in place. Space plays, R toggles repeat, loop a section with the loop controls or A/B/L.

These are the listening sets of grooveback, a project restoring records that survive only as MP3/AAC/Opus rips. Each set below is one experiment: a track with a clean master is compressed to MP3 at a known bitrate, and each method restores that MP3. All tracks of a set hold the same excerpt, so switching between them compares the methods directly.

The six tracks:

  1. Ground Truth — the clean master the MP3 was made from; the reference.
  2. Degraded Input — the MP3 each method receives. Above the codec's cutoff frequency nothing is kept, which the spectrogram shows plainly; below the cutoff the signal survives but degrades — transients smear, and reverb tails and stereo detail thin out.
  3. Apollo — a supervised model trained on MP3-compressed music; it restores the full band in one pass.
  4. A²SB — a diffusion model for bandwidth extension; by design it generates only the band above the cutoff and leaves the band below unchanged.
  5. Apollo → εar-VAE — our chained method: Apollo's output passed through the εar-VAE autoencoder. The autoencoder is trained on clean music only, so the round-trip smooths the artifacts Apollo introduces on heavily degraded input.
  6. εar-VAE − MP3 damage — our latent damage subtraction: the input is encoded, the mean shift that MP3 compression causes in εar-VAE's latent space (measured on a different track) is subtracted, and the result is decoded. It restores spectral balance rather than waveform detail.

The spectrogram belongs to the selected track, and clicking it seeks. Differences are clearest on monitoring headphones or speakers.

codec_32k

A 6 s clip, the test asset from Apollo's release — the brighter of the two sources. At 32 kbps the codec keeps nothing above about 5.5 kHz; everything a method shows above that edge is generated, not recovered. This is the severe case, where Apollo → εar-VAE sounds cleaner than Apollo alone: the round-trip removes the artifacts Apollo introduces at this damage level.

codec_64k

A 6 s clip, the test asset from Apollo's release — the brighter of the two sources. At 64 kbps the kept band reaches about 11 kHz. This is the crossover case: by ear, the verdict splits between Apollo and Apollo → εar-VAE.

codec_128k

A 6 s clip, the test asset from Apollo's release — the brighter of the two sources. At 128 kbps the kept band reaches about 16.5 kHz — the damage level of a typical rip — and most of the remaining damage sits inside the kept band. Here Apollo alone stays closest to the master; the smoothing that helps at 32 kbps moves the output away from it.
