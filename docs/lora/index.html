grooveback listening — LoRA fine-tune and Apollo

Every track is level-matched to −14 LUFS with one common headroom gain per player, master included. Solo is exclusive: keys 1–9 or a click switch tracks in place. Space plays, R toggles repeat, loop a section with the loop controls or A/B/L.

An initial run of grooveback's own restorer: Stable Audio 3 medium-base, fine-tuned with a LoRA for 5 hours on one GPU to turn MP3s back into their masters, next to Apollo, a model trained for the same task. The LoRA trained on fresh windows of the 86 MUSDB18-HQ training songs and on remixes of their stems, as Apollo does. Both restore every 6 s clip of the 50 MUSDB18-HQ test songs, 3,677 clips, at six MP3 bitrates; the figure shows their mean scores against the master. We are now iterating on data augmentation and training strategies.

Apollo scores above the LoRA on all four metrics at every bitrate: at 32 kbps, 11.8 against 7.1 dB SDR, and 9.1 against 12.1 dB LSD. The LoRA beats the untouched MP3 only on LSD, which counts the band above the codec's cutoff that the MP3 lost: 12.1 against 32.0 dB at 32 kbps.

Each player below holds one clip, five ways:

  1. Master — the clean clip; the reference.
  2. MP3 — the master coded at the section's bitrate, which both models receive.
  3. Apollo — Apollo's restoration of the MP3.
  4. LoRA — the LoRA's restoration, Euler sampler at 32 steps.
  5. Master through SA3's autoencoder — the master encoded and decoded by Stable Audio 3's autoencoder, with nothing else done: the closest the LoRA's output can get to the master, since everything it makes goes through that decoder.

For each model and bitrate, the clips come from the 3 songs where its SDR gains most over the MP3 it was given and the 3 where it gains least, one clip per song, the most extreme. A clip can appear more than once. Scores are in dB against the master, measured on these files before level matching. The spectrogram belongs to the selected track, and clicking it seeks.
